How to get rid of a refrigerator, air conditioner, or TV in Boston

Fridges, freezers, air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and old TVs and monitors can't just go out with the trash in Boston. The City says Massachusetts law bans items containing Freon or cathode ray tubes (CRTs) from the trash, so Boston runs a separate special item collection by appointment. If your appliance still works, a few local charities will take it instead.

Can I throw out a fridge, AC, or old TV in Boston?Not with regular trash. The City says Massachusetts law bans items containing Freon or cathode ray tubes (CRTs) from the trash, so Boston collects them through a separate special item collection by appointment (the City page doesn't mention a fee; book online or call 311).
Does Boston pick up old fridges, ACs, or TVs?Yes, by appointment through its special item collection; the City page doesn't mention a fee. Schedule online or call 311 (617-635-4500), and put the item where your trash normally goes by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before. Limits: 5 items per appointment and 10 per calendar year. The City collects from the curb only.
How do I get rid of a fridge for free in Boston?Two ways. The City of Boston's special item collection picks it up from the curb by appointment (311 or 617-635-4500); the City page doesn't mention a fee. If the fridge still works and your electric utility is a Mass Save Sponsor (Cape Light Compact, Eversource, National Grid or Unitil), Mass Save offers a no-cost pickup plus a $75 rebate in 2026 for units 10 to 30 cubic feet inside, and will pick up inside if there is one flight of stairs or fewer.
Do I have to remove the fridge doors?Yes. Remove all doors from fridges and freezers, including mini fridges, and take out all food. Fridges or freezers with doors attached or food inside will not be collected.
Does the City take dishwashers or water heaters?No. Dishwashers, water heaters, and plumbing fixtures are not part of this program; the City says to donate them or hire a private hauler.
Where can I donate a working appliance or TV near Boston?Appliances: the Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore in West Roxbury accepts working appliances less than eight years old, and Household Goods in Acton takes refrigerators, washers, electric dryers, and some air conditioners newer than 10 years. TVs: Household Goods takes flat-screen TVs under 55" and under 10 years old with an attached base and power cord, and the Thrift Shop of Boston takes smart flat-screen TVs but not older, CRT, or rear-projection TVs. Size and age rules are below.
Where can I donate a TV for free near me in Boston?The Salvation Army advertises free donation pickup (1-800-SA-TRUCK) but doesn't take TVs older than 5 years or large console TVs. The Thrift Shop of Boston in Roslindale takes smart flat-screen TVs by drop-off appointment, and Household Goods in Acton takes flat screens under 55" and under 10 years old with an attached base and power cord; neither states a drop-off fee. Old CRT TVs can go to the City's special item collection by appointment through 311.

Which items need the City's special collection

The City lists refrigerators and freezers, water coolers, ice makers, air conditioners, and dehumidifiers (items with Freon), plus televisions, laptops, and computer monitors (items with CRTs). Dishwashers, water heaters, and plumbing fixtures are not part of this program; the City says to donate them or hire a private hauler. The City does not collect commercial-grade items.

How the free City pickup works

Schedule an appointment online or call 311 (617-635-4500); the City does not collect special items without one. Put the item where your trash normally goes (curbside, public alley, or dumpster area) by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before, somewhere publicly accessible and not on private property. The City page doesn't mention a fee. Limits: 5 items per appointment and 10 per calendar year. Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before. If it's missed, call 311 within 24 hours.

Fridge and TV prep rules (or it won't be taken)

Remove all doors from fridges and freezers, including mini fridges, and set the doors next to your trash bin for regular collection. Take out all food. Fridges or freezers with doors attached or food inside will not be collected. Don't smash appliances or electronics. TVs and computers must be freestanding, not boxed, and wipe your data from computers first.

Recycle a working fridge or freezer and get $75 (Mass Save)

Mass Save offers a no-cost pickup and a $75 rebate for an inefficient, working refrigerator or freezer, for Massachusetts electric customers whose Mass Save Sponsor is Cape Light Compact, Eversource, National Grid or Unitil. The unit must be plugged in, cooling, and empty at pickup, measure 10 to 30 cubic feet inside, be owned by you, and be at your home or business; disconnect any water line, and remove all doors before putting it outside. Inside pickup is only available with one flight of stairs or fewer. The limit is two units per equipment type per electric account per year, and pickup must happen in 2026 for the $75. A working dehumidifier picked up with the fridge earns $30. Schedule online or call 866-527-SAVE (7283); the rebate takes 6 to 8 weeks.

Donating a working appliance

The Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore in West Roxbury accepts appliances less than eight years old, including refrigerators, washers, dryers, free-standing stoves, cooktops, and microwaves, and dishwashers only if never installed; it doesn't take in-wall stoves, and everything must work. Household Goods in Acton takes refrigerators, washers, electric dryers, and air conditioners up to 18" x 30" x 24" if newer than 10 years, plus flat-screen TVs under 55" and under 10 years old with an attached base and power cord. The Thrift Shop of Boston takes smart flat-screen TVs and small appliances but not older, CRT, or rear-projection TVs. The Salvation Army's national FAQ says it generally takes working, non-built-in appliances such as ACs, microwaves, TVs, and washers and dryers, but not gas appliances or TVs older than 5 years; rules vary by location, so call 800-SA-TRUCK.

Where to donate a TV for free near Boston

Three donation options near Boston take TVs, each with its own limits. The Salvation Army advertises free donation pickup on satruck.org (1-800-SA-TRUCK); its national FAQ lists TVs among accepted items but not large console TVs or TVs older than 5 years (the date is on the back of the TV), and local rules vary. The Thrift Shop of Boston (33 Corinth Street, Roslindale, 617-325-5300) takes smart flat-screen TVs at drop-off by appointment on Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays, but not older models, rear-projection TVs, CRTs, or TV armoires. Household Goods (530 Main Street, Acton) takes flat-screen TVs with an attached base that are under 55", less than 10 years old, and have their power cord, at drop-off Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays from 9 a.m. to noon. Neither the Thrift Shop of Boston nor Household Goods states a drop-off fee. If no one will take your TV (for example an old CRT set), Boston residents can book the City's special item collection by appointment through 311; the City page doesn't mention a fee.

Paid pickup from inside your home

The City only collects from the curb, so if you can't move the appliance yourself you'll need help. ReSupply lists appliances among the items it picks up for donation in Boston, with teams that handle stairs; it's a paid service with a separate booking fee. LoadUp, a junk removal service, lists TV disposal from $80, fridge disposal from $115, and washer removal from $85 on its national page, with the final price set by ZIP code, item, and pickup conditions. Confirm the price before you book.
