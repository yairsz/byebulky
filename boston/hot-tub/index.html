How to get rid of a hot tub in Boston

An old hot tub is too big and heavy for City trash pickup in Boston: the City's trash pages don't list hot tubs, it says it doesn't collect plumbing fixtures or construction debris, and it won't pick up items on private property. That leaves selling or giving away a working tub, or paying a removal crew. Either way, drain it and get the power disconnected first.

What the City of Boston will and won't collect

The City of Boston's trash and special collection pages don't list hot tubs. The City says it does not collect construction debris, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris, and to find a local private hauler for those. It also says it can't collect items on private property and doesn't take commercial-grade items. Call 311 (617-635-4500) to ask about a specific tub.

Sell or give away a tub that still works

LoadUp says that if a hot tub still works, selling or giving it away is often worth trying first, on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist or local community groups. It warns that the taker needs a way to move several hundred pounds, and that a tub that leaks or hasn't run in years is much harder to give away. 1-800-GOT-JUNK? lists trading it in to a dealer and selling it online or at a yard sale as other ways to get rid of one.

Get it ready: drain, disconnect, clear a path

LoadUp says the tub must be fully drained before pickup, at least a day ahead, and that any electrical, gas or plumbing connections must be disconnected by a licensed professional first. A hardwired 240-volt tub needs a licensed electrician, not just a switched-off breaker; LoadUp puts that typically at $200 to $500 as a separate cost. A plug-in 120-volt portable tub only needs to be unplugged. 1-800-GOT-JUNK?'s prep list adds disassembling the pumps, heaters, power and plumbing, packing accessories separately, and clearing a wide enough path.

Hot tub removal cost: LoadUp from $875, 1-800-GOT-JUNK? by estimate

LoadUp's hot tub page lists in-home removal from $875, curbside from $870, same-day from $885 when booked before noon, and next-day or scheduled up to 90 days ahead from $875. It removes above-ground tubs, portable, inflatable and swim spas, and shells, and says crews cut the shell into sections on site when it won't fit through a gate (standard gates run 32 to 36 inches, it says); a tub built into a deck may need structural work first. 1-800-GOT-JUNK? sends a two-person team for a free, no-obligation in-person estimate, charges by how much truck space the load takes, and says its price includes labor and disposal fees. It also hauls away hot tub covers.

Where the old tub goes

LoadUp says metal parts such as the frame, pump and wiring are recycled where local options allow, while the fiberglass or acrylic shell and foam insulation are harder to recycle and usually go to an approved waste facility. 1-800-GOT-JUNK? says hot tubs are typically not accepted at local landfills or recycling depots and that it tries to recycle or donate them.
