How to get rid of a piano in Boston

Pianos are one of the hardest things to get rid of in Boston. The City's trash and special-collection pages don't mention pianos, and Goodwill and the Thrift Shop of Boston both say they don't take them. Your real choices are to donate a piano that plays well, give it away to someone who pays for the move, or pay a hauler to take it out of your home.

What the City of Boston and local thrift stores say

The City of Boston's trash and special collection pages don't mention pianos. The City says it will not pick up items on private property and doesn't collect commercial-grade items, so check with 311 (617-635-4500) before you put a piano at the curb. Goodwill's Massachusetts donation list says it is unable to accept pianos and organs, and the Thrift Shop of Boston lists pianos among items it does not accept (it takes other musical instruments).

Donate a piano that still plays well

PianosForEducation.org accepts piano donations from all 50 states and reviews acoustic pianos, electronic player or recording pianos, and a few digital pianos in good operational order. Send its Donate Piano form with three close-up photos (model number, serial number, and a front view with the bench), and volunteers reply by email within seven business days. If it accepts, its nationwide pickup vendor schedules a pickup on its next route through your area, which can take a few weeks. In Boston, the Community Music Center of Boston is accepting a limited number of grand pianos in very fine condition for its Nubian Square campus; a CMCB piano faculty member evaluates the piano first, and CMCB says it isn't responsible for transportation.

Give it away to someone who pays for the move

Piano Adoption is a nationwide classifieds site for free pianos, with Greater Boston among its Massachusetts areas. Its give-away ad option costs $19.95, appears immediately, runs 90 days, and you choose who gets the piano. Its FAQ warns that "free" pianos aren't really free for the taker, who pays to move, tune and repair them, and tells takers never to send money to someone offering something for free.

Paid piano removal: what LoadUp and 1-800-GOT-JUNK? charge

LoadUp's piano removal page lists in-home pickup from $430, curbside from $425, same-day from $440 when booked before noon, and next-day or scheduled up to 90 days ahead from $430. It says the price depends on the instrument's size and type and the access, such as a basement or upper floor, and it removes upright, spinet, console, studio, baby grand, grand, digital and electric pianos. 1-800-GOT-JUNK? says piano acceptance varies by city and some pianos may not be removable, so contact the local franchise; it gives a free in-person estimate and charges by how much truck space your items take.
