How to get rid of a couch, mattress or furniture in Boston

Boston has free City options for furniture, mattresses and appliances, but each has its own rules. Here's what each option takes, what it costs (only where they publish it), and where we got the information.

Couch or other furniture?If you get City curbside trash collection, furniture can go out next to your bins on your regular collection day. To donate instead, check the ReStore and furniture banks below; most require clean, undamaged pieces.
Mattress or box spring?Mattresses are banned from Massachusetts trash. In a building with 6 units or fewer, book the City's curbside mattress pickup appointment (the City page mentions no fee). In 7+ units, use your property manager or one of the mattress haulers below.
Fridge, AC, TV or monitor?Book a City special item collection. Remove fridge doors and all food first.
Can't carry it to the curb?City pickups are curbside only. Paid options that come inside include ReSupply (donation pickup), LoadUp, and several mattress haulers.

City bulk item pickup for Boston residents (3)

city bulk pickup

City of Boston curbside furniture collection

Takes
  • Furniture
Condition rules
Put furniture out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day, placed neatly next to your trash receptacles. Trash must be curbside by 6 a.m.; don't put it out before 5 p.m. the evening before. The City does not collect construction debris, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, fences, or auto parts.
Stairs / inside pickup
No. Curbside only; the City will not pick up on private property.
Lead time / hours
Your regular trash collection day (find it in the Trash Day app or the City's schedule search).
Cost
No fee is mentioned on the City page. Check with 311 if unsure.
Service area
Addresses that receive City of Boston curbside trash collection. Large buildings (7+ units) should check the City's large-building page and their property manager.
How to book
Website / booking · 311 or 617-635-4500 · 311@boston.gov

City of Boston curbside mattress & box spring recycling (appointment)

Takes
  • Mattresses (memory foam, innerspring, hybrid, latex)
  • Box springs
Not accepted
  • Futons
  • Mattress toppers
  • Other non-mattress items (these can go out with curbside trash)
Condition rules
Appointment required; mattresses are not collected with regular trash (Massachusetts waste ban). Soiled, dirty, or bed-bug mattresses must be bagged and fully sealed. Do not crush or cut the mattress. Place curbside by 6 a.m. on your appointment day (or after 5 p.m. the night before), not blocked and with nothing on top. Limit: 10 items per calendar year. Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before. Missed pickups must be reported to 311 within 24 hours of the scheduled date.
Stairs / inside pickup
No. Curbside only; items on private property are not collected.
Lead time / hours
Not stated, check with them
Cost
No fee is mentioned on the City page. Check with 311 if unsure.
Service area
City of Boston residents in buildings with 6 units or fewer. Buildings with more units must use their property manager or a private hauler.
How to book
Website / booking · 311 or 617-635-4500 · 311@boston.gov

City of Boston Zero Waste Day (household hazardous waste drop-off)

Takes

  • Propane tanks (empty or full)
  • Motor oil, gasoline, pesticides and other household chemicals
  • Aerosol cans
  • Rechargeable, lithium and car batteries
  • Fluorescent bulbs and other intact mercury items
  • Car and bike tires (8 maximum per person)

Not accepted

  • Construction debris
  • Medical waste

Stairs / inside pickup
No. Drop-off only: you bring the items to the event.
Lead time / hours
2026 Zero Waste Days were May 16, June 27, and August 15; the September 26 event was postponed to Saturday, November 21, 2026, 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m., at 315 Gardner Street, West Roxbury. Don't arrive before 8:30 a.m.; the City stops accepting waste promptly at 12 p.m.

Free (the City's event page lists the price as FREE).

City of Boston residents.
Address
