How to get rid of a refrigerator, washer, AC or TV in Cambridge, MA

Large appliances can go to the curb in Cambridge, but most need a disposal permit first. The City charges $25 per item ($20 if you're 62 or older) for fridges, freezers, washers, dryers, stoves, air conditioners and TVs over 20 inches, and some metal items get a free permit. Here's how the permit works, what needs no permit at all, and the donation and paid options if you'd rather not carry it to the curb.

Quick answers: appliances in Cambridge

Does the City of Cambridge pick up appliances?

Yes, curbside, but you need a disposal permit first. The City's permit page charges $25 per item, or $20 if the resident is 62 or older, for items such as refrigerators and freezers, washers and dryers, stoves, air conditioners, dehumidifiers, water heaters and TVs or monitors over 20 inches. Apply online or in person at DPW, 147 Hampshire Street.

Source: City of Cambridge: furniture, appliance and large item disposal

How much does it cost to get rid of a refrigerator in Cambridge, MA?

The City permit is $25 per item ($20 for residents 62 or older), plus an online convenience fee if you pay by card ($0.99 + 2.99%) or e-check ($5.50); paying in person at DPW avoids it. Remove the doors first. If the fridge still works and your electric utility is a Mass Save Sponsor (Cape Light Compact, Eversource, National Grid or Unitil), Mass Save offers a no-cost pickup and a $75 rebate in 2026 instead.

Source: City of Cambridge: furniture, appliance and large item disposal · City of Cambridge: appliance and large item permit application · Mass Save

When does Cambridge pick up large appliances?

The City says most permit items are scheduled for Wednesdays or Thursdays. Put the item at the curb by 7 a.m. on your collection day, or after 6 p.m. the night before. An item isn't on the collection schedule until all fees are paid.

Source: City of Cambridge: furniture, appliance and large item disposal · City of Cambridge: appliance and large item permit application

Do I need a permit to throw out a TV in Cambridge?

Only for TVs and computer monitors over 20 inches (measured diagonally), which need the $25 permit. Small TVs and monitors of 20 inches or less need no permit: place them close to the curb edge by 7 a.m. on your regular collection day.

Source: City of Cambridge: furniture, appliance and large item disposal

What large items can go out in Cambridge without a permit?

The City lists furniture such as couches and tables (one piece per week on trash day), small electronics such as microwaves, stereos, box fans and small printers, TVs and monitors 20 inches or smaller, one toilet or sink per household on collection day, and carpet or lumber in up to 3 tied bundles of 3' x 3' x 1'.

Source: City of Cambridge: furniture, appliance and large item disposal

Which appliances need a paid permit in Cambridge

The City lists these as needing a permit and payment of a fee: air conditioners, clothes washers and dryers, copiers and printers over 40 lbs, dehumidifiers, refrigerators and freezers (non-commercial, doors removed), stoves, TVs and computer monitors over 20 inches diagonal, trash compactors, water coolers and water heaters, and larger motorized exercise equipment. The City's permit page sets the charge at $25 per item, or $20 if the resident is 62 or older. The City says Freon is removed from air conditioners, dehumidifiers, freezers and refrigerators to meet the Clean Air Act, and all appliances are then sent to a local scrap metal recycling facility.

Items that need a free permit

Some items still need a permit but no payment. The City's main page lists small exercise equipment and bicycles, lawnmowers, radiators, snow blowers, and metal filing cabinets and desks; its permit page lists free pickup for bicycles, metal desks and cabinets, grills, exercise equipment, lawnmowers and snowblowers. You still have to apply before putting them out.

How to get the permit and schedule the pickup

Apply with the City's online Appliance/Large Item Disposal Permit application, or in person at DPW, 147 Hampshire Street (Monday 8:30 a.m. to 8 p.m., Tuesday to Thursday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., Friday 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m.), where you can pay by check, money order, cash or credit card. Paying online adds a convenience fee of $0.99 + 2.99% by card or $5.50 by e-check. An item isn't officially on the collection schedule until all fees are paid. The City says most items are scheduled for Wednesdays or Thursdays; put them at the curb by 7 a.m. on your collection day, or after 6 p.m. the night before. No commercial or industrial items. Questions: 617-349-4800 or theworks@cambridgema.gov.

Small TVs, microwaves and other items with no permit

TVs and computer monitors with a screen of 20 inches or less need no permit; place them close to the curb edge by 7 a.m. on your regular collection day. Small electronics such as microwaves, stereos, box fans and a small printer or fax machine also need no permit. The DPW Recycling Center at 147 Hampshire Street takes small electronics, including small appliances and computers, on Tuesdays and Thursdays from 4 to 7:30 p.m. and Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., but it doesn't take appliances.

Recycle a working fridge or freezer and get $75 (Mass Save)

Mass Save offers a no-cost pickup and a $75 rebate for an inefficient, working refrigerator or freezer, for Massachusetts electric customers whose Mass Save Sponsor is Cape Light Compact, Eversource, National Grid or Unitil. The unit must be plugged in, cooling and empty at pickup and measure 10 to 30 cubic feet inside; disconnect any water line and remove all doors before putting it outside. Inside pickup is only available with one flight of stairs or fewer. Pickup must happen in 2026 for the $75. A working dehumidifier picked up with the fridge earns $30. Schedule online or call 866-527-SAVE (7283); the rebate takes 6 to 8 weeks.

Donating a working appliance

The Habitat for Humanity Greater Boston ReStore in West Roxbury accepts appliances less than eight years old, including refrigerators, washers, dryers, free-standing stoves, cooktops and microwaves, and dishwashers only if never installed; everything must work. Household Goods in Acton takes refrigerators, washers, electric dryers and air conditioners up to 18" x 30" x 24" if newer than 10 years, plus flat-screen TVs under 55" and under 10 years old with an attached base and power cord. Its volunteer pickup doesn't cover Cambridge, so drop off in Acton or use its paid pickup partners.

Paid pickup from inside your home

The City only collects from the curb. LoadUp's Cambridge page lists appliance pickup from $105 and says its loaders carry items out from any room; ReSupply lists appliances among the items it picks up for donation and its Boston page lists Cambridge among the communities it serves, as a paid service with a separate booking fee. Confirm the final price before you book.
