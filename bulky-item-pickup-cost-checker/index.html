One old fridge, six cities around Boston, and six different rules. Four of them post a fee between twenty-five and thirty dollars. Boston's page mentions no fee at all. And Quincy's page doesn't say.

So before you pay a junk hauler to take it, check your City first. All six of these Cities run their own curbside appliance pickup.

Cambridge charges twenty-five dollars, or twenty if you're sixty-two or older, with a permit. Somerville sells a twenty-five dollar sticker. Newton is twenty-five dollars: pay by ten p.m. Sunday and pickup is arranged for Thursday of the following week. Brookline charges thirty, and you schedule a week ahead.

Boston lists fridges under its special item collection, and the City page mentions no fee. In Quincy, call Capitol Waste by three p.m. at least one full business day before your trash day. The City's pages don't give a price, so ask when you call.

Here's the catch. All six City pickups are curbside only, so you have to get the fridge out yourself. Boston says items left on private property are not collected.
